## Temperature Conversion

- The conversion factor $9/5$ (or $1.8$) is based on the difference in the number of degrees between the freezing and boiling points of water on the two scales.
- On the Celsius scale, the freezing point of water is $0°C$ and the boiling point is $100°C$. The difference is $100$ degrees $(100 - 0 = 100)$.
- On the Fahrenheit scale, the freezing point of water is $32°F$ and the boiling point is $212°F$. The difference is $180$ degrees $(212 - 32 = 180)$.
- The ratio of the difference in degrees between the two scales is what gives us the conversion factor.
- $\frac{\text{Fahrenheit}}{\text{Celsius}} = \frac{180}{100} = \frac{18}{10} = \frac{9}{5} = 1.8$
- This means that for every $1$-degree change in Celsius, there is a $1.8$-degree change in Fahrenheit.
- The "$+ 32$" in the formula is an **offset** that accounts for the fact that the two scales don't start at the same zero point. While $0°C$ is the freezing point of water, $0°F$ is not. The freezing point of water in Fahrenheit is $32°F$. You must add this offset after converting the "size" of the degrees.

### Celsius to Fahrenheit

To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, you multiply the Celsius temperature by $9/5$ (or $1.8$) and then add $32$.

$F = (C \times \frac{9}{5}) + 32$

### Fahrenheit to Celsius

To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius, you first subtract $32$ from the Fahrenheit temperature and then multiply the result by $9/5$.

$C = (F - 32) \times \frac{9}{5}$

### Celsius to Kelvin

The Kelvin scale is an absolute temperature scale, meaning $0 K$ is the lowest possible temperature (absolute zero).

To convert Celsius to Kelvin, you add $273.15$ to the Celsius temperature.

$K = C + 273.15$

### Kelvin to Celsius

$C = K - 273.15$
