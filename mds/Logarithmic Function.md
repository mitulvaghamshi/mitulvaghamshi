A logarithm is a mathematical operation that tells you what exponent is needed to produce a certain number. It's essentially the inverse operation of exponentiation.  
  
**Core Concept**  
  
In the equation $b^x = y$, the logarithm answers the question: "To what power ($x$) must we raise the base ($b$) to get the number ($y$)?"  
  
This relationship can be expressed in logarithmic form as $\log_b(y) = x$.  
  
⚬     **b** is the **base** of the logarithm.  
⚬     **y** is the **argument** or **number**.  
⚬     **x** is the **logarithm** or the **exponent**.  
  
For example, since $10^2 = 100$, the logarithm of 100 with base 10 is 2. This is written as $\log_{10}(100) = 2$.  
  
**Common Bases**  
  
Two bases are used so frequently they have their own special notation:  
  
⚬     **Common Logarithm ($\log$)**: This is the logarithm with base 10. If no base is written, it's assumed to be 10. It's often used in fields like chemistry (pH scale) and engineering.  
    ⚬         $\log(100) = 2$ because $10^2 = 100$.  
⚬     **Natural Logarithm ($\ln$)**: This is the logarithm with base $e$ (Euler's number, approximately 2.71828). It's widely used in calculus and other areas of mathematics and science to describe exponential growth and decay processes.  
    ⚬         $\ln(e) = 1$ because $e^1 = e$.  
  
**Key Properties**  
  
Logarithms have several important properties that simplify calculations:  
  
⚬     **Product Rule**: $\log_b(xy) = \log_b(x) + \log_b(y)$  
⚬     **Quotient Rule**: $\log_b(\frac{x}{y}) = \log_b(x) - \log_b(y)$  
⚬     **Power Rule**: $\log_b(x^p) = p \cdot \log_b(x)$  
⚬     **Change of Base Formula**: $\log_b(x) = \frac{\log_a(x)}{\log_a(b)}$ This rule allows you to convert a logarithm from one base to another.  
  
**Real-World Applications**  
  
Logarithms are not just an abstract mathematical concept; they are used to handle very large or very small numbers and scale them into a more manageable range.  
  
⚬     **Richter Scale**: Measures earthquake intensity on a logarithmic scale. A magnitude 7 earthquake is 10 times stronger than a magnitude 6.  
⚬     **pH Scale**: Measures the acidity or alkalinity of a substance. A decrease of 1 on the pH scale represents a tenfold increase in acidity.  
⚬     **Decibels (dB)**: Measures sound intensity and signal strength.  
⚬     **Computer Science**: Logarithmic complexity ($O(\log n)$) describes algorithms that become more efficient as the input size increases, such as binary search.
